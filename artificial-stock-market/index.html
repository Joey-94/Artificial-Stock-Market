01 · What this project is

Overview

This is an agent-based model of a limit-order-book market: traders submit limit and market orders into a continuous double auction with price–time priority, and prices emerge from order flow rather than from an equilibrium equation. Because the market mechanism itself is explicit code, the model can answer questions no reduced-form model can: what happens to volatility, liquidity, and price discovery if we change the trading rules?

The engine has carried two lines of research. During my internship at the Shenzhen Stock Exchange Research Institute (Financial Innovation Lab), it was configured to replicate ChiNext trading rules — no daily price limits for new listings, T+1 settlement, circuit-breaker halts — and used to evaluate proposed reforms of the price cage, the rule that restricts limit-order prices to a band around the prevailing quotes. Separately, the variant documented here adds a listed corporation agent that learns, by reinforcement, whether to report earnings truthfully or manage them.

Attribution — what I wrote vs. what I built on

my implementation  The agent behavioral models: HBPlayer, the heterogeneous-belief trader (503 lines), and Corporation, the learning disclosure agent (235 lines) — rewritten on top of the lab's simulation framework.

Working inside an existing architecture, respecting its invariants, was part of the exercise.

03 · Agent design

Traders with heterogeneous beliefs

The trader population is grounded in survey evidence on Chinese retail investors, in which fundamental, trend-following, and short-horizon traders coexist in comparable proportions. Rather than three discrete types, each of the 1,500 agents blends three signals with its own weights — a fundamentalist term (log-gap between perceived fundamental value and price), a chartist term (moving-average momentum), and idiosyncratic noise:

r̂i,t = g1,i · ln( vi,t / pt ) + g2,i · ( MAτ/4 / MAτ − 1 ) + gn,i · εi,t

The weights g1, g2, gn are random draws, normalized per agent — the population spans a continuum from near-pure fundamentalists to near-pure chartists. The same draws scale each agent's risk aversion and investment horizon τ: the more chartist an agent, the shorter its horizon, the more frequently it re-enters the market (Poisson arrivals with intensity 1/τ), and the sooner its orders expire. Heterogeneity is structural, not decorative.

src/model/agents/HBPlayer.javaforecast rule (excerpt)
// fundamentalist component: log-gap between perceived value and price
double fundamentalR = Math.log(pf / pt);

// chartist component: short-run vs long-run moving average of past prices
pShort = pShort / length1;              // MA over  τ/4 ticks
pLong  = pLong  / length2;              // MA over  τ   ticks
meanR  = pShort / pLong - 1;

// blend with agent-specific weights + noise
forecastR = g1 * fundamentalR + g2 * meanR + gn * epsilon;
forecastP = pt * Math.exp(forecastR);

Forecasts map to target positions through a wealth constraint; the gap between target and current holdings becomes an order. Whether it enters as a passive limit order or crosses the spread as a market order depends on where the agent's price sits relative to the book — this is what generates realistic two-sided order flow:

ConditionSideOrder type
price < ask, target > heldbuylimit order joins the book below the ask
price ≥ ask, target > heldbuymarket order — lifts the ask immediately
price ≤ bid, target < heldsellmarket order — hits the bid immediately
price > bid, target < heldselllimit order joins the book above the bid

Agents also manage inventory: stochastic cancellation of stale orders, expiration after τ ticks, and mark-to-market wealth updates on every fill.

04 · Policy application

Stress-testing the ChiNext price cage

Under ChiNext rules, a limit buy may not be priced above 102% of its benchmark quote, and a limit sell not below 98% — the "price cage." Orders outside the band are parked by the exchange host and re-activated when prices move into range. The rule is meant to stop fat-finger errors and momentum ignition, but in mid-2020 a newly listed stock showed that with a thin order book the cage itself can chase a jumping benchmark price upward and let extreme trades through.

The simulation asked a question that cannot be answered from historical data alone, because the counterfactual mechanisms have never existed: which redesign of the cage actually prevents these jumps? Two results carried the policy analysis. The stress scenario reproduced the real-world jump under both the current mechanism and the reject variant — evidence that the parking rule, widely suspected at the time, was not the direct cause. Only the second, closed band anchored to the last trade price contained the jump: it breaks the feedback loop between a moving quote benchmark and the orders it admits, at negligible cost to volatility, liquidity, or pricing efficiency in normal conditions.

05 · Second research line

A firm that learns to manage earnings

The variant in this repository replaces the exogenous news process with a strategic Corporation agent. Every reporting date it observes the true earnings innovation and chooses between reporting truthfully or smoothing — report half, bank the rest. The choice is a logit over the running average utility each strategy has earned:

src/model/agents/Corporation.javastrategy choice (excerpt)
// softmax choice between earnings management (EM) and truth-telling
double pEM = Math.exp(beta * earningsManagement)
           / (Math.exp(beta * earningsManagement) + Math.exp(beta * truthTelling));
EM = Math.random() < pEM;

// utility of last period's action: risk-adjusted log price growth
rate    = Math.log(avP1 / avP2);        // avg price this cycle vs last
utility = rate - 0.5 * rho * variance;  // mean–variance criterion
utility = -Math.exp(-rho * utility);    // CARA utility

// running-average reinforcement update of the chosen strategy's value
earningsManagement = (earningsManagement * emTimes + utility) / (++emTimes);

Two institutional details give the model its bite. Smoothing accumulates: the gap between real and reported earnings builds up until it breaches a bound, forcing a "big bath" — a corrective disclosure that dumps the distortion and hands the strategy a painful utility draw. And investors are heterogeneously sophisticated: each trader sees through managed earnings only with a skill-dependent probability, so the same report shifts different traders' perceived fundamentals differently — feeding disagreement straight back into the order book.

06 · From simulation to evidence

Experiment pipeline

Configure
All parameters externalized in setups/main.properties (belief-weight scales, horizons τ, tick size, agent census) — scenarios are reproducible and sweepable without recompiling.
Simulate
One tick = one minute; 240 ticks per trading day; multi-hundred-day runs after burn-in. A batch wrapper repeats runs across randomized parameter draws; a Swing GUI (JFreeChart) shows live price, return, volume, and book-depth charts during development.
Record
Per tick: fundamental value, bid/ask close, trade price, volume, 40-level depth on each side, eight order-flow counters (buy/sell × limit/market × count/quantity), and strategy-state variables.
Validate
MATLAB scripts test simulated returns against the stylized facts of real markets — the calibration gate every scenario must pass before its counterfactuals mean anything. Baseline runs: return kurtosis 3.94 (fat tails), Hurst exponents of 0.58 / 0.70 / 0.75 for absolute returns, midquote returns, and spread (long memory, all > 0.5), and autocorrelation of absolute returns decaying slowly over 30+ lags (volatility clustering) — consistent with A-share data.
